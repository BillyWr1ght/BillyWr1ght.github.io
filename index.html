About Me

William Wright

"Curiosity drives my projects—and shapes how I approach every new challenge."

I'm a student driven by curiosity and a genuine interest in how things work. I like taking an idea from a rough question to something I can research, design, build, and share. Engineering is one of my biggest interests, but this portfolio is also a place for the skills, experiences, and goals that shape who I am.

A lot of my project time is spent in Onshape and SolidWorks—not just sketching parts, but thinking through assemblies, tolerances, and motion. That work has shown me how design can connect physics and math with creativity, patience, and practical problem-solving.

I document projects and experiences carefully because reflecting on what worked, what failed, and what I learned helps me grow. Every setback, revision, and new challenge gives me something useful to carry into the next project.

The Project

Making every part work together

The goal is to build a lightweight arm that moves reliably. Each joint has to balance strength, weight, servo torque, printability, and range of motion.

The project is developed as one connected system: model, print, assemble, test, and revise.

Design Process

From model to motion

01

Model

Plan the joint layout, link geometry, servo placement, and clearances in CAD.

02

Build

Print the parts, inspect their fit, and assemble the servos and fasteners.

03

Refine

Test motion under load and revise any joint that flexes, binds, or lacks torque.

Working as a Team

The Robotics Club has taught me that strong teamwork depends on more than technical skill—it requires communication, shared responsibility, adaptability, and bringing together work created by different people.

On the team, I focus primarily on mechanical design and CAD — creating parts, assemblies, and drawings that the rest of the team can use to build and modify. I also support the programming team by documenting the mechanical constraints that affect their software design.
